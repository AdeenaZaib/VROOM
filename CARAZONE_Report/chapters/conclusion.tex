\chapter{Conclusion}
% ============================================================

This project addresses intrusion detection for the TSN Ethernet backbone of
automotive zonal architectures, targeting attacks against TSN protocol features that
degrade timing guarantees without altering packet content or traffic volume. The
contribution is the use of protocol-native, schedule-derived signals as the detection
feature space, trained federatively across zone controllers.

At the mid-report stage the data generation path is complete and reproducible: a
four-zone TSN topology with gPTP synchronisation, three instrumented attack
configurations, fifteen seeded runs and a labelled dataset of 4{,}740 feature windows.
Delay-attack injection has been validated against its expected theoretical signature.

The project's central premise has been tested ahead of schedule and holds. Features
computed within a single zone detect the attack no better than a majority-class
predictor; two cross-zone residual features reach 0.95--0.98 macro-F1, detecting two
synchronisation intervals after onset with no missed attack windows in the
best-performing arm. The gain is $+0.43$ to $+0.49$ macro-F1 and is insensitive to model
choice, which is the evidence the contribution statement requires.

Two negative results bound the claim. Oscillator drift manipulation is not realisable as
a runtime attack under the simulation framework, which reduced the committed attack set
to two classes. More significantly, no fixed threshold on the offset residual separates attack from
benign behaviour. The synchronisation convergence transient at run start produces
residuals 2.7 times larger than the attack, and because those windows constitute
roughly 11\% of benign data, no percentile-based cut-off succeeds either. The benign
class is a mixture of a convergence population and a steady-state population with the
attack lying between them in magnitude, which is what makes a learned detector necessary
rather than merely convenient, and what makes the second residual feature, weak on its
own, the one that separates the two benign populations.

The remaining work is the detection model, the federated training pipeline and the
comparative evaluation. The plan sequences a central decision gate, the comparison
between protocol-native and generic traffic features, ahead of federated
infrastructure, so that the project's primary claim is tested before further work is
built upon it.
